Due Process
  • Due process comes from the Bill of Rights. It protects people accused of breaking a law and people in jail for breaking laws.
  • Accused means blamed or charged with doing something wrong.
  • Rights of people accused: be considered innocent until proven guilty; be told what law they are accused of breaking; be present at their trial; defend themselves at a fair trial.
  • Rights of people found guilty: the consequence must be similar to what others got for the same law, and it must be fair and reasonable.
  • The punishment must fit the crime. A fine for riding a bike on the sidewalk is reasonable. Six months in jail for it is not.
Amendment Process
  • The framers (creators) wrote the Constitution in 1787. It is a living document, which means it can be changed.
  • Amend means change. Ratify means approve.
  • It is hard on purpose, so one person or one group can't change it. The process makes citizens part of it.
  • There have been 27 amendments in nearly 240 years.

Voting Rights Amendments
AmendmentYearWhat it changed
15th1870African American men, including formerly enslaved men, can vote. Passed after the Civil War. Some states then used poll taxes and literacy tests to block them.
19th1920All women can vote, thanks to the Suffrage Movement. Women waited 144 years.
23rd1961Washington, D.C. gets electors in the Electoral College, so its people have a voice in choosing the president.
24th1964Ended poll taxes (fees to vote).
26th1971Voting age lowered from 21 to 18, because 18-year-olds could be drafted.
